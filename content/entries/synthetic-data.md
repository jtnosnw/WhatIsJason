## Plain

Real-world data is expensive to collect and sometimes scarce, sensitive, or messy. Synthetic data sidesteps that by generating training examples artificially — often by having another AI model produce them — rather than gathering them from the real world. It's increasingly used to fill gaps in training data, especially for rare situations that don't show up often enough in real data alone.

The obvious risk is that synthetic data can inherit or even amplify the flaws of whatever generated it.

## Technical

Synthetic data is programmatically or model-generated rather than collected from real-world sources, used to augment training sets where real data is scarce, imbalanced, expensive, or privacy-sensitive. Common generation methods include using a strong model to produce labeled examples, simulation environments, or data augmentation pipelines. A known risk is model collapse or quality degradation when models are trained too heavily on synthetic data generated by earlier models, compounding errors over generations.

## Examples

- Using a strong language model to generate labeled training examples for a smaller model
- Simulated sensor data used to train self-driving perception systems
- Synthetic conversations generated to fill gaps in a customer-support fine-tuning dataset
